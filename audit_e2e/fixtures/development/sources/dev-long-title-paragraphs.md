# River Access Maintenance Review
## Overview
This review records inspection routines, completed work, gaps, and a July follow-up for the east-bank path.
## Purpose
This internal review records how the maintenance schedule changed after responsibility for the east-bank footpath moved from the parks unit to the riverside operations team. The note is intended to help the new team understand the current schedule before it proposes any changes.
## Why the handoff changed the inspection rhythm for the east-bank path
Before the handoff, parks staff walked the route every second Tuesday and filed a short checklist at the end of the shift. Operations staff inherited the route in February and added a visual check after heavy rain. The two routines overlap, but they inspect different failure points: the first catches loose rail fasteners and the second checks runoff channels and standing water.
## Work completed since February
The team replaced six rail fasteners, cleared three drainage grates, and repainted two distance markers. A second inspection found one grate blocked again after leaf fall. The work log records completed work separately from observations that still need a crew visit.
## Open questions for the next review
The route has no fixed rainfall threshold for an extra inspection. The parks unit used local judgment, while the operations checklist asks staff to record rain only as light, moderate, or heavy. A shared threshold would make the two logs easier to compare, but this source does not set one.
## Evidence limits
The February-to-May log covers twelve scheduled walks and four weather-triggered checks. It does not contain visitor counts, injury reports, or a cost comparison with the prior year. Any conclusion about safety or budget impact would need another source.
## Next review date
The operations lead will review the combined checklist with parks staff on 14 July 2027. The meeting agenda includes route ownership, rainfall wording, and whether the repeat grate blockage needs a separate work order.
